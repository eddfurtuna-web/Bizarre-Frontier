The race

In Classic mode, 100 riders race from San Diego to New York over 9 stages. Every finish scores points (100 for a stage win). The rider with the most points after the New York stage wins the purse, unless only one rider is left alive first. Everything happens on its own: you watch, follow riders and tweak the settings.

Before the race you can choose up to 5 legends on the starting poster: type a name (like "Johnny" or "Pocoloco"), click it, and that legend is guaranteed a spot. Your picks stay until you remove them.

Two modes on the starting poster. Classic: 100 riders, 9 stages, points decide the winner. Endless: one long straight trail with no finish line, raced until only one rider is left alive. Pick anywhere from 50 to 500 riders with the slider. Vitality Herbs and corpse parts turn up much more often, new Devil's Palms keep opening, and riders grow more violent the longer the race runs.

The screen

  • Chips above the map: riders left, berserk chance, day and time, weather, terrain, loose corpse parts, the leader, and who the camera is watching.
  • The map: the thin strip at the top is the minimap of the whole stage (the red ring is a Devil's Palm). The black ticker under the map repeats the latest event.
  • Watch Party (top right): comments from spectators reacting to big moments.
  • Leaderboard: stage order or overall points, with HP pips. Search it by rider, Stand or family.
  • Events / Stand Codex / Champions tabs: the full event log with filters, every Stand and its awakened form (searchable), and the Hall of Champions.

Controls

  • Start / Pause, and the speed buttons ½×, 1×, 2×, 4×, 8×.
  • Click a rider on the map to follow them; Follow leader goes back to the front. Drag the map to look around freely; scroll or use − / + to zoom.
  • Double-click a leaderboard row to open that rider's profile: look, Stand, HP, family, horse, items and grudges.
  • In a profile, press ♡ Add to favorites. Favorites get a pink ring and name tag on the map, a pink arrow at the edge of the map when they're off-screen, a ♥ on the leaderboard and minimap, and a Favorites chip above the map. If a favorite dies, a banner tells you right away.
  • Music / Sound / Vol for audio, and Berserk to set how violent the race gets (or turn it off).
  • ★ Moments (and ★ Best moments on the winner's poster) opens auto-recorded replays of the race's biggest moments: kills, time stops, John Rod, awakenings, D4C doubles, barrage rushes, stage wins and more. The best ten are kept; click one to watch it again. The killer is tagged in red.
  • New riders rerolls the whole field. Esc closes windows like this one.

Fights

  • Guns: riders carry a revolver (200 m), a rifle (480 m, accurate), a shotgun (130 m, sprays pellets that can hit twice up close or clip a bystander), a lever-action carbine (320 m, sometimes fires twice) or twin revolvers (always two shots, less accurate). You can see a rider's gun in their profile.
  • Every rider has 3 HP (automobiles have 5). A bullet, Stand hit or hard ram costs 1. At 0 they fall.
  • Every 2 seconds each rider has a small berserk chance (it multiplies each stage by the Growth setting: ×1, ×1.25, ×1.5, ×2 or ×5). Berserk riders shoot, ram, or use their Stand on someone nearby. The victim, and their family or posse, can fight back.
  • Riders hold grudges: anyone who shot at or hurt them is remembered, and a rider going berserk will often go after the person they hate most if they're in range.
  • Riders sometimes talk: they can intimidate someone (who backs off), befriend them (and bring them into the family), or just chat. Every talk shapes their relationships: acquaintances, friends, rivals, and now and then sweethearts. A few couples start the race together; lovers never shoot each other and grieve (and seek revenge) when one falls. Open a rider's profile and pick the Relationships tab to see everyone they're on good or bad terms with.
  • Time stops and time erase work in a bubble around the user (about 450 m for Star Platinum, 650 m for The World, 900 m for Over Heaven, 600 m for King Crimson and 800 m for its Requiem). Riders outside keep racing as normal; only those inside are affected, and the effect is only drawn inside the circle.
  • King Crimson + Epitaph erases time: for 2.5 seconds everyone inside the crimson bubble plays on, but when it ends they all snap back as if nothing happened, including deaths. Only the user's own moves and hits remain.
  • The World Requiem (The World awakened by a corpse part) keeps the 2.5-second stop, but anyone frozen in it loses all momentum when time resumes. If a Requiem user then absorbs the Saint's Heart, it ascends to The World: Over Heaven (5-second stop, bigger bubble). Heaven Ascension DIO starts with it.
  • Tusk grows by killing: 2 kills with ACT1 unlock ACT2, and 3 kills with ACT2 unlock ACT3. An ACT3 user who absorbs a corpse part has a vision: everyone freezes, they ride ahead of the pack and meet the Saint, who grants Tusk ACT4.
  • Gold Experience Requiem can return almost anything to zero: attacks, gunshots, Stand abilities, overtakes, time stops, erased time, stage wins, meteors, new items, even deaths. The map is sucked into a galaxy for a moment ("You Will Never Reach The Truth"), then it's as if the thing never happened. It needs a minute to recover each time.
  • Mandom rewinds the whole world about 6 seconds (12 when awakened), its user included, with a red flash and a backwards-spinning watch. Log entries from the rewound seconds get crossed out. Soft & Wet: Go Beyond throws a spinning bubble that erases one rider outright.
  • Barrage rushes: when two Stand users are close, a berserk one may start a rush. Their Stands trade flurries of fists (or bullets, goo, fire, ice or sparks for Stands that don't punch), the two riders are dragged together, and every second one of them may win the exchange and deal 1 damage. The stronger Stand wins more often.
  • D4C pulls doubles of nearby riders out of parallel worlds. A double joins the D4C user's family or posse and fights for them, but it rots away at the end of the stage. If a double ever touches the rider it was copied from, both explode.
  • Made in Heaven's stage reset now also undoes every death and wound from that stage, and can be used again after a 40-second cooldown. Its awakened form (at most once every 10 minutes of racing) resets the whole race back to the first stage: points wiped, and everyone who ever died is alive again. Only its user remembers.
  • Now and then, John Rod drops out of the sky, walks up to one rider and tears them apart. Nothing can stop him, and he vanishes with a glint.

Stands and legends

A few riders carry Stands. Legends (gold trim, ★) are canon characters who always bring their own Stand; Pocoloco and Heaven Ascension DIO are the rarest of all. A Stand appears behind its user only while it's being used. A rider's profile shows their Stand's stat chart (Power, Speed, Range, Durability, Precision, Potential, graded A to E), which improves when the Stand awakens. Each Stand can awaken into a stronger form; the Codex tab lists them all.

Saint's Corpse and the Devil's Palm

Glowing corpse parts appear ahead of the pack (with a bell and "IT APPEARS AGAIN"). Riders chase them; riding over one starts absorbing it. It can merge and grant a Stand (some parts lean toward certain Stands, like the Heart and D4C, the Left Arm and Tusk, the Rib Cage and The World) or awaken an existing one. It can also reject its holder. Sometimes a meteor falls and opens a Devil's Palm (the big red crater): corpse parts rise there more often, and just riding through it can, very rarely, awaken a Stand.

Riders whose Stand is already awakened still chase corpse parts. The part may reject them; if it doesn't, it is stored inside them instead of evolving anything, and every 3 stored parts give +1 max HP.

On the trail

  • Items: Vitality Herbs heal, a Stone Mask makes a vampire, a Steel Ball teaches the Spin.
  • Riders and horses: every horse has a name (like "Silver Bullet"), riders wear all sorts of hats, coats and trinkets, and when a rider falls you'll see their last words.
  • The stages are one long trail: the next stage is already laid out past each finish line. After the break, riders trot across the old finish, form up at the next starting line, and ride on. The old stage fades away once the next finish line is reached.
  • Terrain and weather change speed: rivers need bridges, creeks and beach shallows are waded slowly, cliffs must be ridden around, ridges need ropes, quicksand and mud slow you, storms and snow slow everyone. There are also random events like stampedes, twisters, gold and wildfire.
  • Towns sometimes line the trail: saloons, stores and shacks behind the fences, with townsfolk strolling the boardwalks. Curious ones wander onto the trail to watch, and scatter when horses come. The slow ones get trampled.
  • Now and then the trail passes a landmark: the Grand Canyon, Delicate Arch, Devils Tower, Old Faithful, the Grand Prismatic Spring, the standing stones of Maryhill, Niagara Falls, Monument Valley or the Statue of Liberty. The camera leans over to show it when the rider you're watching is near.
  • Some stages bend gently left or right, and the fences and the train track follow the curve. Grassy biomes have patches of long grass that sway in the wind and slow riders a little, and every so often an American flag hangs from the fence.
  • A day lasts about 30 seconds and a night about 20, with smooth sunrise and sunset. The soundtrack has 10 original tracks.
  • The final stage runs down the main street of New York; buildings line both sides, so everyone has to ride through the middle.

Reading the map

  • Dashed white circle: the rider you're following. Pink ring and ♥: your favorites. Gold outline and ★: legends. Red ring: a rider who has gone berserk. 1. 2. 3. tags: the current top three.
  • Blue strips: rivers (look for the wooden bridges; bold riders swim) and narrow creeks you can wade. Turquoise water on beach stages: long shallows everyone has to splash through slowly.
  • Brown rock walls are cliffs: riders steer around them, or through the gap of a canyon narrows (the striped marker shows the open end). Ridges with ropes slow everyone for a climb.
  • Slipstream: a horse tucked in 25–80 m behind another rider is shielded from the wind and runs about 4.5% faster (7.5% in high wind or a dust storm). You'll see faint white wind streaks behind drafting riders. Riders tend to drift in behind whoever is just ahead, so trailing riders always have a way to catch up. On-foot runners and the automobile don't draft.
  • Fighting styles: a Steel Ball (item) teaches the Zeppeli Spin, a fighting style like vampirism. Spin users never carry a gun; they throw steel balls instead. Ball Breaker and Wrecking Ball are Stands whose balls ignore every defense, counter and protection.
  • Some Stands now act on their own when awakened: Purple Haze's virus hops from rider to rider, Catch the Rainbow carries a storm bubble, Bad Company's army marches beside its user, Chariot Requiem swaps souls ("The Requiem Quietly Plays..."), Scary Monsters turns riders into dinosaurs, and many more. Check the Stand Codex for each one. Black Sabbath's arrow can awaken a Stand in anyone it stabs (20%). Whitesnake needs two corpse parts to become Made in Heaven. Iggy is a dog.
  • If a rider ever gets wedged against a cliff, ridge or obstacle and barely moves for 2 seconds, they work themselves free and slip past it.
  • Wooden signs along the fences announce each obstacle, and the Next chip above the map says what's coming up for the rider you're watching and how far away it is.
  • The minimap strip shows the whole stage: riders as dots, rivers and shallows in blue, cliffs in brown, the Devil's Palm as a red ring.
  • Glowing bones are corpse parts; small green, grey and silver pickups are items. Speech bubbles are riders talking, or their last words.
  • Clear days bring soft sunrays; rain, storms, fog, snow, dust and wind all slow riders a little.

Bizarre Nature

The Bizarre Nature button next to the title opens a second game mode (heavily work in progress): a JoJo life simulator in the town of Morioh. Pick how many people live there and which legends move in, then watch them work, eat, fall in love, hold grudges, post on MoriohNet, fight with their Stands and get chased by the police. The race pauses while you're there; ← Bizarre Frontier brings you back.

Champions and honours

The winner gets the purse in a finish cutscene, then the Honourable Riders wall shows five standouts (runner-up, most damage, most corpse parts, and more). Press Close on the wall to see the final standings (you can watch the Best moments from there). Winners are saved in the Champions tab and may come back to race again, but a champion who dies in a race is greyed out and never returns.

How Bizarre Nature works

Bizarre Nature is a life simulator set in Morioh. Everything runs on its own: people wake up, go to work or school, eat, drink coffee, go for walks, hang out at home, fall in love, hold grudges, and sometimes kill each other. You watch, follow and read what happens.

Controls

  • Drag to pan, scroll or − / + to zoom, click a person or car to open their profile. Zoom in close and roofs turn see-through so you can watch people inside their homes (or turn on X-ray roofs).
  • ♡ Favorite anyone from their profile: favorites get a pink ring, a filter in People and MoriohNet, and a banner if they die.
  • Stand vision off shows the town the way ordinary people see it: attacks come from nowhere.
  • One in-game hour lasts 30 seconds at 1×. ← Bizarre Frontier takes you back to the race.

People

  • Everyone has a home with its own furnished interior: TVs, game consoles, kitchens, baths, desks with computers, bookshelves, instruments and pets. At home they watch TV, play games, cook, bathe, read manga, practise hobbies and post online.
  • Jobs: office workers, shop clerks, cooks, baristas, bartenders, teachers, doctors, fishers, police officers, students and retirees.
  • Feelings react to what happens: loneliness makes people sad, fights make them angry, being attacked or seeing violence makes them afraid, killing someone brings guilt, anxiety and fear of the police, love makes them happy, work stresses them out. Feelings change what they do next.
  • Anger meters: every relationship has one. Insults, jealousy, grudges and harm fill it. At the breaking point the two start fighting, and every blow traded drains their anger. Killing the other person empties it, then the anxiety sets in.
  • Relationships: family, lovers, best friends, friends, acquaintances, rivals and enemies.

MoriohNet

  • Townspeople post about their day, what they saw, how they feel and who they're angry at. Others like and comment. The town newspaper and the police have accounts too.

Stands

  • Stands come from Bizarre Frontier. Only Stand users can see them; ordinary people can only report an "invisible attacker".
  • Stands can stop time, erase time, heal, plant bombs, rewind, bind, detect villains, vanish, speed up or spread plagues. Stand users smash through walls when chasing someone into (or out of) a building. Crazy Diamond fixes the damage.
  • A strange Arrow turns up now and then. Touch it and you may gain a Stand, get badly hurt, or awaken your Stand into its Requiem form.

Crime and the police

  • Villains appear: robbers, muggers, serial killers, arsonists, Stand rampages and joyriders. Villain legends hide in plain sight.
  • Police patrol, answer reports, chase suspects on foot or with sirens, shoot when they must and make arrests. Jailed people sit in the cells at the station.
  • People who drink too much may drive anyway. Drunk and reckless drivers swerve, speed, run people over and crash into other cars.
